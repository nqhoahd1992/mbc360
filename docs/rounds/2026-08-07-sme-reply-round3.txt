Part A — Tier assignment
A1. Tiering rule
Your rule is broadly correct.
Please retain the following interpretation:
Mandatory: always hard-blocks the gate.
Conditional: hard-blocks only when its defined trigger applies.
Supporting: does not automatically block, but unresolved risk or missing context may require a warning, action or Proceed with Conditions decision.
The tier assignments in your table are accepted, with two adjustments:
Gate 12 – Change-control links: change from Supporting to Conditional. It becomes mandatory where a complaint, post-market finding, CAPA, formula change, artwork change, safety signal or improvement action has generated a Change Control record.
Gate 12 – Market feedback: may remain Supporting for routine lifecycle review, but becomes Conditional where the project has launched and a scheduled post-market review is due.
All other assignments are accepted.

Part D — Decisions already built
D1. Prepared, reviewed and approved sign-off
The current implementation is not sufficient.
Owner + Evidence link is not equivalent to Prepared, Reviewed and Approved sign-off.
Each gate should have distinct recorded roles:
Prepared by
Reviewed by
Approved by
For each sign-off, capture:
authenticated user;
role;
date/time;
decision;
record version;
comment where required.
This should hard-block the gate decision.
The phase-level sign-off should remain as an additional phase-closure approval and should not be replaced by the per-gate sign-offs.
Where risk is low, the same person may prepare multiple gate records, but the reviewer or approver should be independent for safety-, regulatory-, claims- or release-critical decisions.
D2. Claim-support and Published Information Approval rules
Claim ID linkage
Every external product-benefit, safety, efficacy, performance or suitability statement should be required to link to a Claim ID.
Linkage may remain optional only for genuinely non-product corporate information that contains no product claim or technical statement.
Picker restrictions
Developing or Pending claims should be selectable.
The purpose is to document the intended claim early.
However, Pending claims must not allow:
Approved for Release;
Released;
final artwork approval;
external publication.
Wording adaptation
Do not enforce an absolute character-for-character lock across every channel.
The system should retain:
master approved wording;
proposed channel wording;
comparison/review status;
reviewer approval.
Minor adaptation may be allowed where the meaning, scope, qualifiers and evidence burden remain unchanged.
Any material change must create a new or revised claim record.
Automated similarity checking may be used as a warning, but final equivalence must be confirmed by an authorised reviewer.
Release block
Confirmed. A linked claim must be Supported and approved for the relevant SKU, formula version, market and channel before content can reach Approved for Release or Released.

E3(b) Gate 11 Change Control
The existing soft-lock mechanism is suitable for low- or medium-risk open changes.
At Gate 11, apply the following:
Critical or launch-impacting open Change Control: hard-block launch.
Formula, artwork, claims, safety, regulatory, packaging or release-impacting change: hard-block unless implementation and verification are complete.
Low-risk administrative change: may permit Proceed with Conditions following authorised acknowledgement.
Completed, rejected, cancelled or superseded change: does not block if final disposition is recorded.
Therefore, Gate 11 requires more than a duplicate warning. It must evaluate the impact classification and closure status of each open Change Control.
These decisions should complete the current readiness-rule questions.
