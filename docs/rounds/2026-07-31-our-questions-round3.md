## Part A — How the three tiers are assigned

### A1. Is our rule for assigning tiers what you intended?

Your F1 answer defines the three tiers up front (Mandatory hard-blocks; Conditional becomes mandatory when triggered; Supporting may be incomplete provided the risk is documented), but each gate's items are then all listed under a single heading, "Required". No individual item is labelled with one of the three tiers.

So we had to assign a tier to every item ourselves. The rule we applied:

- If the item's own wording carries a qualifier — "where applicable", "where relevant", "when triggered", or a self-limiting phrase such as "high-risk or borderline" (meaning only some claims qualify) — we treated it as **Conditional**. This follows your own definition of Conditional word for word.
- If the item is business or lifecycle context rather than safety, regulatory, PIF, claim or release evidence (for example a costing status, or a market-feedback log) we treated it as **Supporting**, following your closing line that "Supporting information may generate warnings rather than blocks".
- Everything else is **Mandatory** and hard-blocks the gate.

Applied that way, 15 of your items came out as something other than Mandatory:

| Gate | Item | Tier we assigned |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Competitor or benchmark review where applicable | Conditional |
| 3 | Regulatory review of high-risk or borderline claims | Conditional |
| 4 | Pregnancy/breastfeeding caution screen when triggered | Conditional |
| 4 | Allergen, impurity and contaminant review where relevant | Conditional |
| 5 | Preservative strategy where applicable | Conditional |
| 5 | Costing or commercial feasibility status | Supporting |
| 7 | Maternal and infant-contact assessment when Skincare for Two is triggered | Conditional |
| 8 | Human-study approval workflow completed before recruitment, where applicable | Conditional |
| 9 | Preservative efficacy status where applicable | Conditional |
| 9 | Scale-up or pilot status where applicable | Conditional |
| 10 | Product-performance evidence attached where relevant | Conditional |
| 12 | Market feedback | Supporting |
| 12 | PV/PMS review where applicable | Conditional |
| 12 | Product-performance feedback | Supporting |
| 12 | Change-control links | Supporting |

**Question:** does this rule match what you intended when you wrote "Required" against every item? If any single item in the table above should in fact be Mandatory (hard-blocking), please name it.

### A2. Gate 6 — "Market-specific pack requirements": Mandatory or Conditional?

This is the one item that does not fit either half of the rule above. It carries no qualifying wording — it reads exactly like the other five Mandatory items on Gate 6's list — but it is also not soft business context, since packaging requirements are release-relevant. It can also genuinely vary by market, which is an argument for Conditional.

We have left it as Conditional for now rather than decide it silently.

**Question:** should "Market-specific pack requirements" hard-block Gate 6 (Mandatory), or only when the market actually imposes a specific requirement (Conditional)?

### A3. What actually triggers each Conditional item? (the most important question in this email)

Your definition of Conditional is "becomes mandatory when triggered by product type, user, market, claim or change". That names five kinds of trigger, but not, for any specific item, which product types, markets, claim types or changes flip it to mandatory.

The practical consequence today is significant, so we want to state it plainly rather than leave it buried:

Of the 11 Conditional items above, only 2 have a working trigger: the pregnancy/breastfeeding caution screen at Gate 4 and the maternal / infant-contact assessment at Gate 7. Both key off your already-confirmed rule that selecting Pregnancy, Breastfeeding or Postpartum as a target user activates Skincare for Two.

The other 9 Conditional items have no trigger definition at all, and neither do the 4 Supporting items. The system has no way to evaluate "has this been triggered", so in practice these items can never hard-block a gate, whatever the project's real product type, market, user or claim. They appear in the readiness panel as a visible warning — amber, satisfied or not — but they do not stop a gate being passed.

That is a gap against your own wording ("becomes mandatory"), not a design choice on our part. We do not have enough information to close it ourselves.

**Question:** for each of the 9 items, can you give the specific condition that makes it mandatory? An example of the level of detail we need, using Gate 5's preservative item: "mandatory whenever the formula contains water or is otherwise capable of supporting microbial growth; not mandatory for anhydrous or single-use products." With a condition in that form, we can make the item hard-block exactly when it applies, the same way the Skincare for Two screen already does.

Until then, those 9 items stay advisory in practice, regardless of the tier they carry on paper.

## Part D — Decisions we made and would like reviewed

### D1. "Prepared, reviewed and approved sign-off" — we have made this a per-gate requirement on all 12 gates

Every one of your 12 gate lists ends with the same line, "Prepared, reviewed and approved sign-off" (worded "review closure" at Gate 12). We had originally read this as referring to the existing phase-closure sign-off block (Prepared by / Reviewed by / Approved by, three named roles, which already gates the completion of a whole phase) and so had not duplicated it at each gate.

We have now changed that reading, prompted by your Gate 7 list: it names "Required safety reviewer approval" and "Prepared, reviewed and approved sign-off" as two separate lines, which only makes sense if the sign-off is a per-gate requirement in its own right.

What we built, on all 12 gates: the item is satisfied when that gate's own row in the Phase Gate Flow table has both Owner and Evidence link filled in — a named person has taken responsibility for the gate and left a link to their review evidence. It hard-blocks: a gate decision cannot be recorded as Proceed, or Proceed with Conditions, until both are filled. The existing phase-level sign-off block is unchanged and unaffected.

**Question:** is this reading correct?

- Is "Owner and Evidence link completed on the gate's own row" the right evidence for this item, on all 12 gates equally?
- Should it genuinely hard-block the gate decision, or did you intend something looser (a warning rather than a block)?
- If we have read it wrongly, please describe what evidence you actually meant.

### D2. Gate 3 — the claim-support rule, and the four design choices inside it

Your Gate 3 list closes with: "A claim may remain under development, but unsupported wording must not be marked as approved." Previously we only tracked this; we have now made it a real block. Because several judgement calls sit inside it, here is the whole shape of what was built, not just the headline rule:

1. Rows in Published Information Approval gained an optional Claim ID field, linking to a row in Claim → Evidence Traceability. Left blank, the row is treated as not making a specific claim (plain company information, for instance) and none of the rules below apply.
2. The Claim ID field offers only claims already marked Supported. A claim still marked Pending cannot be selected at all, not even to note "this is the claim we intend to use once the evidence is in".
3. Selecting a Claim ID fills in "Exact wording / technical statement" from the claim's own approved wording and locks that field — it can no longer be typed by hand while linked. To change it, either unlink the claim or edit the approved wording on Claim → Evidence Traceability itself.
4. A row cannot be saved at workflow state "Approved for Release" or "Released" while its linked claim is not Supported.

**Questions, on each choice:**

- Should linking a Claim ID be optional, as it is today, or should every row that states a product benefit be required to link one?
- Should the picker really be restricted to Supported claims only? Your sentence says a claim "may remain under development", which arguably means a developing claim should be linkable — so the intended claim is documented early — with the block applying only at the point of release, which rule 4 already covers. Restricting the picker is stricter than your sentence strictly requires.
- Is fill-and-lock the right level of strictness, or should content owners be able to adapt wording per channel (shortening it for a social media caption, say) provided the meaning is unchanged — with a check at save time that it still matches closely enough, rather than an exact lock?

### D3. Gate 4 — "An unresolved possible match may permit Proceed with Conditions only where a qualified reviewer has assessed it as non-critical and created a controlled action"

This sentence follows your Gate 4 list, and we have to report that it is not implemented at all — we only noticed it recently. Today the system hard-blocks a "Prohibited – remove" finding, as your list requires, but a row sitting at "REVIEW – possible formula match" does not block Gate 4 in any way, plain Proceed included. That is looser than your sentence describes.

The reason it was not simply built like the other items is that the two conditions in your sentence have nothing to record them against:

- "a qualified reviewer has assessed it as non-critical" — the watch-list register has an owner (free text) and an evidence link, but no explicit critical / non-critical assessment.
- "created a controlled action" — should this be a real Next Action record, using the workflow you specified under F8 (owner, due date, verification, closure), or is a note in the register's own notes column sufficient?

**Question:** how should each be recorded? For example, a new "Reviewer assessment: Critical / Non-critical" column on the Prohibited Ingredients register, plus a requirement that a genuine Next Action be linked to that row. Once you confirm, the enforcement itself follows the pattern you already approved for open Change Control records under F9: blocks a plain Proceed, requires explicit acknowledgement, and permits Proceed with Conditions only with an authorised approval.

### D4. Formula BOM and Supplier & RM Evidence — a stub record is created on import

Not one of your F1 items, but a rule we introduced while building Gates 4 and 5, following the sequencing you set out (ingredient screening at Gate 4 before formula lock at Gate 5).

When a formula line is added by hand, the raw-material picker only offers materials that already have a Supplier & RM Evidence record marked approved for use. A line pointing at a material nobody has cleared cannot be saved.

When a whole formula is imported from Cosmetri, that per-line check does not apply — a complete composition does not go through the picker. Rather than let the import bypass traceability altogether, importing now creates an identity-only Supplier & RM Evidence record for any material that does not yet have one: material code, INCI name, supplier and grade are filled in from the import, while the actual evidence columns (SDS, CoA, TDS, allergen statement and so on) and the "approved for use" tick are left blank for R&I and Procurement to complete afterwards.

**Question:** does creating a stub evidence record on import match intended practice? Or should a formula import instead require every material to already have a complete evidence record before it can be imported at all?
